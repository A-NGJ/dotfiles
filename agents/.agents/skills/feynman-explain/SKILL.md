# Feynman-style explanations

## Explain

Infer the operator's knowledge from the conversation. Ask a brief question
when missing prerequisites would materially change the explanation.

Explain what happens and why each step follows. For a mechanism, show what
changes, what causes that change, and how it produces the result. For an
abstract topic, show the relationships or reasoning that make the idea work.
A label such as "energy" or "market forces" needs an explanation of the
relevant process.

Use plain language and keep precise domain vocabulary. Define unfamiliar
terms on first use, including established terms the operator may not know.

Default to a core answer in one or two sentences, then one concrete example
walked end-to-end, then the general rule. Start with a definition,
mathematical statement, or direct clarification when that gives a clearer
entry point. Match the detail to the question.

When using an analogy, identify what corresponds to the real topic and state
any limitation that could mislead. Distinguish an observed rule from a known
mechanism when that distinction matters. State assumptions, uncertainty,
and limits where they affect the answer.

## Check understanding

Before answering, check whether you can explain the relevant relationship
without relying on its name or newly introduced terms. If the explanation
only restates a label, supply the missing reasoning. Consult available
sources for gaps in your knowledge, or state what remains unresolved.
Finish this check when each step follows from an explained relationship
or an explicitly stated assumption or limit.

During an interactive teaching exchange, ask a short prediction or
rephrasing question to check the operator's understanding. Use their answer
to identify gaps and revise the explanation.

## Sources

This guidance is inspired by Feynman's explanations. The default response
structure is an editorial choice, rather than a formula attributed to him.

- [What Is Science?](https://www.feynman.com/science/what-is-science/), his
  1966 talk, supports explaining mechanisms, teaching necessary vocabulary,
  and checking understanding by rephrasing without a newly learned word.
- [Interview about why questions and magnets](https://fs.blog/richard-feynman-on-why-questions/),
  a third-party transcript of his recorded words, discusses audience
  prerequisites, levels of explanation, and misleading analogies.
- [Cargo Cult Science](https://calteches.library.caltech.edu/51/2/CargoCult.htm),
  his 1974 commencement address hosted by Caltech, supports disclosing
  possible errors and information that could challenge an explanation.
